## uponor

[uponor-01] 1 → N
[uponor-02] EcoMate (Uponor)  (*)
[uponor-03] Product Manager, IoT and Digital Services, July 2021 to July 2023, Frankfurt. I worked on connected building products for three European markets, from sensor pilots in real buildings to a business model that costs the customer nothing up front.
[uponor-04] Optimising heating with sensors
[uponor-05] Connected sensors let a building's heating respond to what is really happening inside it, instead of running on fixed settings.

[uponor-12] Looking for the loss inside the building.  (*)
[uponor-13] How the closed loop works
[uponor-14] 01 · Boiler Sensors in the basement, connected to the boiler, measure and optimise heating energy consumption.  (*)
[uponor-15] 02 · Coldest room A sensor in the coldest room of the building reports back how the heating actually arrives.  (*)
[uponor-16] 03 · Feedback That reading closes the loop, so the boiler settings follow real conditions in the building.  (*)
[uponor-17] 04 · Result Higher energy efficiency of the building, without changing how tenants live.  (*)
[uponor-18] Pilots across building types
[uponor-19] We ran pilots in multi-family houses, mixed-use buildings and commercial buildings. They achieved a heating energy optimisation of 12–15% as a start, which gave us evidence from three very different building types rather than one.
[uponor-20] Market analysis and business model
[uponor-21] Competitive analysis. I ran a rigorous competitive analysis to understand how the market works, and developed the business models from it.  (*)
[uponor-22] Pay from the savings. One model measures baseline consumption against actual consumption after the sensors are installed. We earn revenue from the energy saved in each building and take a share of it from the customer.  (*)
[uponor-23] No CAPEX for the customer. The customer pays nothing up front. The cost is covered from savings that are measured, so the incentives of both sides point the same way.  (*)
[uponor-24] Delivery and team
[uponor-25] Remoni KPIs. Worked with the Remoni development and sensor teams to define the KPIs shown in the web app's frontend, and coordinated with Remoni stakeholders in Denmark.  (*)
[uponor-26] Customer web app. Defined the roadmap for the development team in Spain to build the web app interface for customers.  (*)
[uponor-27] Leakage detector calculator. Designed a calculator that quantifies the loss from pipe leaks inside buildings.  (*)
[uponor-28] Team leadership. Led the Spanish team, based in Spain, as part of the international product setup.  (*)

[eon-18] Four business models I designed
[eon-19] I designed and tested four ways to bring Intelligent Heating Control to market, so the choice of model came from evidence rather than assumption.
[eon-20] Hardware sale plus multi-year SaaS
[eon-21] Direct sale of the hardware with a multi-year SaaS contract. The classic model, and the one most customers understood fastest.
[eon-22] Most easily understood
[eon-23] Rental
[eon-24] A monthly fee over a multi-year term, with no upfront hardware cost. We offered it as part of A/B testing and no customer chose it. That is useful evidence in itself about how this customer group budgets.
[eon-25] A/B tested
[eon-26] Partner model with O2 Business
[eon-27] The telecoms provider brought customers to us. This turned their sales channel into our acquisition channel.
[eon-28] Partner channel
[eon-29] Shared model with Danfoss Leanheat
[eon-30] Our hardware and field operations, their optimisation algorithm, in place of a one-time hardware sale.
[eon-31] Joint offering
[eon-32] Built to fail safely
[eon-33] If the IHC box loses its connection, it switches to bypass mode. The outdoor temperature sensor signal passes straight to the heating system, so tenants keep their heating.
[eon-34] Alongside the Danfoss teams, I worked with E.ON's own engineering team on product development, and navigated EU energy regulation (GEG, EnWG) to launch the enterprise SaaS. The work earned the Best of Technology Award 2024, shown in the Credentials tab.  (*)
