The PCMS engine

The Product Compatibility Mapping System cross-references a home against 450+ plug-and-play devices and returns bundles that are known to work together.

  1. InputHome attributes, infrastructure layout and budget constraints.
  2. MappingPCMS checks every candidate against the full compatibility database.
  3. OutputBest-rated, fully compatible device bundles for heating, lighting and security.

EcoMate (Uponor)

Product Manager, IoT and Digital Services, July 2021 to July 2023, Frankfurt. I worked on connected building products for three European markets, from sensor pilots in real buildings to a business model that costs the customer nothing up front.

Optimising heating with sensors

Connected sensors let a building's heating respond to what is really happening inside it, instead of running on fixed settings.

12–15%
heating energy optimisation in the first pilots
3
building types piloted: multi-family, mixed and commercial
3
European markets served

How the closed loop works

  1. 01 · BoilerSensors in the basement, connected to the boiler, measure and optimise heating energy consumption.
  2. 02 · Coldest roomA sensor in the coldest room of the building reports back how the heating actually arrives.
  3. 03 · FeedbackThat reading closes the loop, so the boiler settings follow real conditions in the building.
  4. 04 · ResultHigher energy efficiency of the building, without changing how tenants live.

Pilots across building types

We ran pilots in multi-family houses, mixed-use buildings and commercial buildings. They achieved a heating energy optimisation of 12–15% as a start, which gave us evidence from three very different building types rather than one.

Market analysis and business model

  • Competitive analysis. I ran a rigorous competitive analysis to understand how the market works, and developed the business models from it.
  • Pay from the savings. One model measures baseline consumption against actual consumption after the sensors are installed. We earn revenue from the energy saved in each building and take a share of it from the customer.
  • No CAPEX for the customer. The customer pays nothing up front. The cost is covered from savings that are measured, so the incentives of both sides point the same way.

Delivery and team

  • Remoni KPIs. Worked with the Remoni development and sensor teams to define the KPIs shown in the web app's frontend, and coordinated with Remoni stakeholders in Denmark.
  • Customer web app. Defined the roadmap for the development team in Spain to build the web app interface for customers.
  • Leakage detector calculator. Designed a calculator that quantifies the loss from pipe leaks inside buildings.
  • Team leadership. Led the Spanish team, based in Spain, as part of the international product setup.

Four business models I designed

I designed and tested four ways to bring Intelligent Heating Control to market, so the choice of model came from evidence rather than assumption.

  1. MODEL 01

    Hardware sale plus multi-year SaaS

    Direct sale of the hardware with a multi-year SaaS contract. The classic model, and the one most customers understood fastest.

    Most easily understood
  2. MODEL 02

    Rental

    A monthly fee over a multi-year term, with no upfront hardware cost. We offered it as part of A/B testing and no customer chose it. That is useful evidence in itself about how this customer group budgets.

    A/B tested
  3. MODEL 03

    Partner model with O2 Business

    The telecoms provider brought customers to us. This turned their sales channel into our acquisition channel.

    Partner channel
  4. MODEL 04

    Shared model with Danfoss Leanheat

    Our hardware and field operations, their optimisation algorithm, in place of a one-time hardware sale.

    Joint offering

Built to fail safely

If the IHC box loses its connection, it switches to bypass mode. The outdoor temperature sensor signal passes straight to the heating system, so tenants keep their heating.

Alongside the Danfoss teams, I worked with E.ON's own engineering team on product development, and navigated EU energy regulation (GEG, EnWG) to launch the enterprise SaaS. The work earned the Best of Technology Award 2024, shown in the Credentials tab.

Heat Pump Copilot

A commissioning and connectivity copilot for installers at small and mid-sized heat pump vendors entering the German market. It tackles two field problems: fault codes that installers cannot resolve on site, and controller connection drops that customers mistake for hardware failure.

Retrieval over manufacturer manuals, a rules-based commissioning completeness checker, an early-warning tool for drops in heat pump efficiency (COP), tracing with LangSmith and an LLM-as-judge evaluation inside the app. Round 1 covered market sizing and a Tableau dashboard. Round 2 covered EU AI Act classification, GDPR, ROI and risk, and deployment strategy.

School Book Order Bot

An n8n workflow that takes school book orders from a family bookshop's Telegram chat.
